I was there when the MIST rolled in, thick and suffocating, swallowing the small coastal town whole. The old LIGHTHOUSE keeper, a man with a perpetually somber aura, had vanished without a trace, his breakfast still warm on the table. No note, no struggle, just an empty COTTAGE and the unsettling silence that clung to the air like a shroud. The local constable, a man of few words, looked utterly bewildered, his eyes scanning the swirling grey for answers that simply weren't there.

Every single person I spoke with seemed to have an alibi that was just a little too perfect, their eyes holding secrets as deep and dark as the ocean trench nearby. The tide, usually a roaring beast, was unnaturally calm that morning, almost as if it too was holding its breath, waiting for something, anything, to break the eerie spell. Footprints led to the edge of the CLIFF, then abruptly ceased, leaving me with a chilling sense of dread and an undeniable feeling that something otherworldly had occurred.

I spent weeks pouring over old MAPS, interviewing the townsfolk again and again, searching for any scrap of evidence that could unravel this bizarre puzzle. The truth, when it finally coalesced, was far more disturbing than any abduction or simple foul play; it was as if the keeper had never truly existed at all, merely a figment of our collective imagination, a ghost story given flesh, a temporary inhabitant of a place designed to confuse; he was never gone because he was never truly here, swallowed by the very MIST that rolled in.
